The problem

Migrating a live MQ estate means holding
four guarantees true — simultaneously.

No message loss

Every in-flight message must be delivered — none stranded on the old queue, none duplicated on the new one.

No application change

Producers and consumers can't change connection strings or restart. They must not even notice.

No collateral impact

Migrating one app off a shared QM must not disturb the co-tenant apps still running on it.

Full auditability

Every change recorded, causally ordered, and reversible — so it survives a real internal audit.

Any one is easy. Any two are manageable. IntelliAI 2.0 is the control plane that holds all four at once — on a running estate.

The mathematics

Two models — each where it genuinely applies.

Little's Law — drain prediction

L = λW  →  Tdrain ≈ L₀ / μ
depth at cutover L₀ = 1,240 msgs · consumer rate μ = 95/s → predicted drain ≈ 13s

Once the producer is rewired away, no new messages arrive — the arrival rate is zero by construction, so the law's condition holds exactly. It predicts the wait for the operator; the engine still proves the drain by polling depth to a confirmed zero.

Little (1961), Operations Research 9(3)

Absorbing Markov chain — reliability

N = (I − Q)⁻¹

The state machine is an absorbing chain — COMPLETED, ROLLED_BACK, ROLLBACK_FAILED absorb; the rest are transient. The fundamental matrix gives expected steps to absorption and the probability of each outcome. Shown alongside — never merged with — a separate empirical estimate from the real audit log. Model and measurement, never conflated.

Kemeny & Snell (1960), Finite Markov Chains
